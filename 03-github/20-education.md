## What is GitHub Education

GitHub Education is a set of free programs run by GitHub for students, teachers and schools. The goal is to give learners access to professional tools while they are still studying so they can build real skills before entering the job market.

**The main offerings:**

- **Student Developer Pack** - 100+ free tools for verified students
- **Teacher Benefits** - GitHub Team plan free for verified teachers
- **GitHub Classroom** - a platform for distributing and grading coding assignments
- **Campus Expert Program** - leadership and community-building training for student leaders
- **GitHub Octernships** - paid industry placements for students

Everything in GitHub Education is free for eligible individuals. Nothing requires a credit card as a student or teacher.

---

## The Student Developer Pack

The Student Developer Pack is the most valuable free resource available to any student studying technology. It is a collection of professional tools, platforms and services contributed by partner companies who want students to learn their products.

The total stated value is approximately $45,000 to $100,000+ depending on which tools you use. The majority of that is cloud credits that are most valuable if you build at scale. The day-to-day tools - Copilot Pro, JetBrains IDEs, domain names, monitoring tools - are immediately useful regardless of project size.

Benefits last for 2 years from approval and can be renewed while you are still a student.

## How to Apply for the Student Developer Pack

**Requirements:**

- A GitHub account (free to create)
- Currently enrolled at an accredited school, college or university
- Aged 13 or older

**Step-by-step application:**

**Step 1.** Go to `education.github.com/pack`.

**Step 2.** Click **Get Student Benefits**.

**Step 3.** Sign in to your GitHub account if not already signed in.

**Step 4.** Select **Student** when asked your role.

**Step 5.** Enter the name of your school. GitHub has a database of institutions - start typing and select from the dropdown. If your school does not appear, type the full name anyway.

**Step 6.** Provide your school email address. An address ending in `.ac.uk` (UK), `.edu` (US), `.ac` or your institution's own domain (e.g. `@university.edu.ng`, `@student.unilag.edu.ng`) is the fastest route. GitHub sends a verification email - open it and click the confirmation link.

**Step 7.** If GitHub cannot automatically verify your institution from the email domain, it prompts you to upload a document as proof of enrolment. Acceptable documents:

- **Student ID card** - must show your full name, the institution's name and either an expiry date or a "valid through" date that is in the future. Photo must be clear and unobstructed.
- **Official enrolment letter** - on institutional letterhead, showing your name, the current academic year and the institution name. Must be signed or stamped.
- **Tuition payment receipt** - showing the current academic year, your name and the institution name.
- **Class schedule or transcript** - official document showing you are registered for the current term.

**Step 8.** Submit the application. GitHub reviews it manually if automatic verification fails. This typically takes 1 to 3 business days. You receive an email when approved or when more information is needed.

**After approval:**

- Go to `education.github.com/pack` to see all available offers
- GitHub Copilot Pro activates automatically on your GitHub account - no separate redemption needed
- Every other offer requires you to click through to the partner and redeem it separately
- Redemption links are on the Pack page next to each offer

## How to Redeem Individual Offers

Each offer requires a separate step. The following covers the highest-value ones.

**GitHub Copilot Pro:**

No action needed. Once your Student Developer Pack is approved, Copilot Pro activates on your account automatically. Confirm it at `github.com/settings/copilot`.

**JetBrains IDEs:**

1. On the Pack page, find the JetBrains offer and click **Get access**
2. You are redirected to JetBrains' Student Licence page
3. Click **Apply now** under "Free individual licences"
4. Select **I am a student** and verify with your GitHub account
5. JetBrains emails you a licence activation link
6. Download any JetBrains IDE from `jetbrains.com/toolbox-app` (install the Toolbox App first - it manages all IDE installations)
7. Open any JetBrains IDE and activate with your licence

**Microsoft Azure:**

1. On the Pack page, click the Azure offer
2. You are directed to Azure for Students at `azure.microsoft.com/en-us/free/students`
3. Click **Start free** and sign in with your GitHub account or create a Microsoft account
4. Verify your student status. GitHub education approval counts.
5. $100 in credits are added to your Azure account immediately. Access Azure at `portal.azure.com`.

**DigitalOcean:**

1. Click the DigitalOcean offer on the Pack page
2. Create a DigitalOcean account using the link provided (standard sign-up)
3. $200 in credits are applied automatically to new accounts registered through the Student Pack link
4. Access your dashboard at `cloud.digitalocean.com`

**Namecheap domain:**

1. Click the Namecheap offer on the Pack page
2. You are redirected to a Namecheap page for GitHub students
3. Search for a `.me` domain (one free `.me` registration is included)
4. Sign in or create a Namecheap account
5. The domain registers at $0 for the first year. SSL certificate is also free for the first year.

**Frontend Masters:**

1. Click the Frontend Masters offer on the Pack page
2. You are redirected to Frontend Masters with a coupon pre-applied
3. Sign up for a new Frontend Masters account
4. 6 months of Pro access is activated. After 6 months, your account reverts to the free tier unless you pay.

## Troubleshooting Your Application

**"We could not verify your academic affiliation"**

GitHub could not automatically confirm your school from the email domain. Solutions:

- Check the verification email in your inbox - click the confirmation link if you have not already
- Make sure you used your institution email, not a personal address
- Upload a student ID or enrolment letter as described above

**Uploaded document was rejected:**

- The document must be legible - photograph in good lighting with the full document in frame
- It must show your institution name, your name and a date confirming current enrolment
- Accepted formats: JPEG, PNG, PDF. Maximum file size: 8 MB.
- Do not crop out any part of the document - show the whole thing

**Application keeps asking for a document even with a school email:**

Some institution email domains are not in GitHub's database. This is common for newer universities, polytechnics and institutions in some countries. Upload a student ID - it will work.

**Email verification link expired:**

GitHub's email verification links expire after a short time. Go back to `education.github.com/pack` and restart the application to generate a new verification email.

**Application is stuck in review for more than 5 days:**

Contact GitHub Support at `support.github.com` with your application details. Include your GitHub username and the name of your institution.

**I have a school email but my institution is not in the dropdown:**

Type the full institution name manually and submit. GitHub's manual review team can verify institutions not yet in the database.

## What Happens When Benefits Expire

**Duration:** Student Developer Pack benefits last for 2 years from the date of approval.

**Renewal:** While you are still an enrolled student, you can renew at `education.github.com`. Submit fresh proof of enrolment - a student ID valid for the new academic year is sufficient.

**On expiry without renewal:**

- **GitHub Copilot** reverts to the Free tier (2000 completions, 50 chat messages per month)
- **JetBrains IDEs** revert to the Community editions (PyCharm Community, IntelliJ IDEA Community - still free but without professional features). Any Professional edition IDE will ask you to purchase a licence.
- **Cloud credits** (Azure, DigitalOcean) expire based on the partner's own terms - typically 12 months from redemption, not 2 years
- **Domains** need renewing at full price after the first year
- **Other partner offers** expire on their own individual schedules - check each partner's terms

**After graduation:**

Benefits are for active students. After graduating you are no longer eligible to renew. Some partners offer graduate or professional discounts - JetBrains, for example, offers a 25% discount to recent graduates in their first year of work. Check each partner's site individually.

## GitHub Classroom

GitHub Classroom is a free web application at `classroom.github.com` that lets teachers automate the distribution, collection and assessment of coding assignments using GitHub repositories.

**How it works from the student's perspective:**

1. Your teacher shares an assignment link
2. You click the link and sign in with your GitHub account
3. GitHub Classroom automatically creates a private repository for you, pre-populated with any starter code the teacher set up
4. You work on the code and push commits as you would normally
5. The teacher can see all student repositories and any automated test results from their Classroom dashboard

**How it works from the teacher's perspective:**

1. Create a classroom at `classroom.github.com` linked to a GitHub organisation
2. Create an assignment, link it to a template repository containing starter code and instructions
3. Set a deadline if desired
4. Copy the assignment link and share it with students
5. All student repositories appear in your Classroom dashboard with commit activity and test results

## Setting Up a GitHub Classroom Assignment

**Prerequisites:** A GitHub organisation to link the classroom to. Create one at `github.com/organizations/new`.

**Step 1.** Go to `classroom.github.com` and sign in.

**Step 2.** Click **New classroom**.

**Step 3.** Select the GitHub organisation to link it to. All assignment repositories will be created inside this organisation.

**Step 4.** Name the classroom (e.g. "EECS2024-Lab" or "CS101-Spring2025").

**Step 5.** Optionally invite students directly, or share the classroom link and let students join themselves.

**Step 6.** Click **New assignment** inside the classroom.

**Step 7.** Configure the assignment:

- **Assignment title** - e.g. "Lab 3: Binary Search Trees"
- **Deadline** - optional; students can still submit after the deadline but the dashboard flags it as late
- **Repository visibility** - Private (students cannot see each other's work) or Public
- **Template repository** - select or create a repository containing the starter code and a `README.md` with instructions. If you have no template, create a new empty repository.

**Step 8.** Set up autograding if desired (see next section).

**Step 9.** Click **Create assignment**.

**Step 10.** Copy the assignment invitation link and share it with students. Each student clicks it once to create their repository.

**Group assignments:**

When creating an assignment, select **Group assignment** instead of Individual. Students form groups by creating or joining a named group when they click the invitation link. Each group gets one shared repository.

## Autograding in GitHub Classroom

Autograding runs automated tests on student code every time they push a commit. Results appear on the Classroom dashboard immediately.

**Setting up autograding:**

1. When creating or editing an assignment, scroll to **Add autograding tests**
2. Click **Add test**
3. Choose a test type:
   - **Run command** - runs a shell command and checks the exit code (0 = pass, non-zero = fail)
   - **Run command, check output** - runs a command and checks that stdout matches expected output exactly
   - **Input/Output** - provide input and expected output; Classroom runs your program and compares

**Example autograding configuration for Python:**

```
Test name: Unit tests
Test type: Run command
Command: python -m pytest tests/ -v
Timeout: 30 seconds
Points: 100
```

**Example for C:**

```
Test name: Compile
Test type: Run command
Command: gcc -o solution solution.c && echo "Compiled successfully"
Timeout: 10 seconds
Points: 20

Test name: Test case 1
Test type: Input/Output
Input: 5
Expected output: 120
Command: ./solution
Points: 40
```

**Autograding runs on:** Every push the student makes. GitHub Actions is used under the hood - each push triggers a workflow that runs the tests. Results are reported back to the Classroom dashboard showing pass/fail per test and total score.

**Feedback pull requests:**

Classroom automatically creates a pull request called "Feedback" on each student repository. Teachers leave code review comments on this PR. Students can see and respond to comments. This mirrors the professional code review workflow students will use in industry.

> [!TIP]
> Even as a student, if your university uses GitHub Classroom, understanding how it works helps you. Your assignment repository is a private copy of the teacher's template. Your commits are visible to your teacher and any teaching assistants. The feedback PR is where to look for review comments.

## Campus Expert Program

GitHub Campus Experts are student leaders who build technical communities at their universities. They run workshops, hackathons and study groups, represent GitHub in their academic community and receive training and funding to do so.

**What Campus Experts receive:**

- Training curriculum covering public speaking, technical education, workshop facilitation and community building
- GitHub Campus Expert certification badge on their GitHub profile
- Travel grants and funding for running community events (conferences, hackathons, meetups)
- Event grant funding to cover venue, catering and materials costs
- Access to GitHub staff for mentorship and support
- Membership in a global network of Campus Experts across 50+ countries
- Early access to GitHub product betas and insider updates

**Eligibility requirements:**

- Currently enrolled student at a university or college
- At least 18 years old
- Basic proficiency with Git and GitHub
- Demonstrated interest in building community (evidence required)

**What counts as demonstrated community interest:**

- Running or co-founding a university tech club, coding society or hackathon team
- Organising or presenting at a tech event on campus
- Maintaining an active online presence in the developer community (blog, open source contributions, YouTube channel)
- Volunteering at or helping organise external tech events

**Application process:**

Applications open once or twice per year. The process includes a written application form, a short video introduction (typically 1 to 3 minutes describing yourself and your community goals) and a review by GitHub Education staff.

Acceptance is selective - approximately 100 to 200 new Campus Experts are added per cohort globally from a much larger applicant pool.

**Apply at:** `education.github.com/experts`

> [!NOTE]
> If you are not yet ready to apply, the right approach is to build something visible first. Run one workshop at your university. Start contributing to open source. Organise a small study group. Come back with concrete things you have done, not just things you plan to do.

## GitHub Octernships

GitHub Octernships are paid industry placements (internships) for students. They are run in partnership with companies that want to hire developer talent.

**What they are:**

Octernships are project-based internships typically lasting 2 to 4 months, done remotely. Each Octernship has a specific project scope agreed between the company and GitHub. Students apply for specific Octernships, complete a technical application task and if selected work directly with the company.

**Pay:** Octernships are paid. The exact amount varies per company and project but is competitive with standard internship rates.

**How to apply:**

1. Go to `github.com/readme/octernships`
2. Browse open Octernship listings
3. Each listing has a technical application task - complete it in a public GitHub repository
4. Submit the repository link through the listing before the deadline

**Eligibility:** Open to students globally. Specific listings may have additional requirements (language, region, technical stack).

## Common Mistakes

**Using a personal Gmail or Outlook address.** GitHub cannot automatically verify student status from a personal email. Use your institution email.

**Uploading an expired or future-dated student ID.** The document must confirm you are enrolled right now. An ID that shows an expiry date that has already passed will be rejected.

**Not renewing.** Benefits last 2 years. Set a calendar reminder to re-apply before they expire, while you are still enrolled.

**Forgetting to redeem individual offers.** Pack approval does not automatically give you everything. You must click each offer and follow the partner's redemption steps separately.

**Waiting until graduation to apply.** Apply as early as possible. You can benefit from JetBrains, Copilot, cloud credits, domains and more across your entire course. Students who apply in their first week get 2 to 3 years of value. Students who apply in final year get months.

**Not checking offer terms.** Cloud credits expire after 12 months regardless of the 2-year pack duration. Read each offer's terms to know when it expires and whether there are usage limits.

**Applying during a gap year or leave of absence.** GitHub requires active enrolment. Applying while on a gap year, leave or deferral may result in rejection or later benefit revocation.

## Summary

- The **Student Developer Pack** gives verified students free access to 100+ tools including Copilot Pro, JetBrains IDEs, cloud credits, domain names and monitoring tools
